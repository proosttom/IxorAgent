Why trust matters
Agentic AI can be smart, scalable, and autonomous, but it will fail without trust. No 
matter how capable a system is, users won’t adopt it if they don’t feel comfortable 
handing over control. Trust isn’t just a bonus; it’s the foundation for real-world adoption.
As agents become more independent, the challenge changes: it’s no longer just about 
proving that a system works, but showing it can be trusted. Without trust, even the 
most advanced AI becomes shelfware. 
Trust is what turns a working prototype into a daily tool. It’s what makes people  
delegate tasks, rely on outcomes, and keep using the system - especially when they 
don’t fully understand how it works.
This Impact Paper explores how to build that trust through transparency, predictability, 
and respectful design.

What we mean by trust
In Agentic AI, trust is not only about the 
agent itself, but also about the ecosystem 
around it.
Users feel safe when the agent provides 
valid answers, not just socially acceptable 
ones designed to please, but responses 
that are genuinely useful and reliable.  
 
Trust also grows when the interaction 
feels natural and the agent clearly fits the 
business context.
Equally important is what happens behind 
the scenes: the team and the architecture. 
Knowing that experts have designed, 
host, and monitor the system makes trust 
sustainable.

Why trust is harder in Agentic AI
How to build trust into Agentic AI
Agentic systems don’t just respond to input: they plan, take action, use tools,  
and adapt. That flexibility is powerful, but also introduces complexity.
Key challenges include:
• Unpredictability: Small context changes can lead to very different outcomes.
• Hidden decisions: Memory, tools, and intermediate steps happen behind the 
scenes.
• Lack of control: Users may not know how to intervene or redirect the agent.
• Shifting behaviour: Learning systems may evolve in ways that confuse or unsettle 
users.
Classic software earns trust through reliability. Agentic AI must also manage  
initiative and uncertainty, making the trust challenge more dynamic and nuanced.
1. Be transparent, but relevant
Don’t flood users with logs or token counts. Instead, offer timely, useful cues -  
like what the agent plans to do next, or why it picked a certain path.
2. Design for predictability
Use consistent language, outputs, and flows. Predictable agents feel smarter  
because they create less friction. Surprises break confidence.
3. Respect boundaries
Don’t assume consent. Confirm before high-impact actions. Let users pause, reverse, 
or review what’s happening. Respect in design builds respect in use.
4. Show confidence levels
Let users know when the agent isn’t sure. Don’t fake confidence.
5. Give users control points
Autonomy doesn’t mean loss of control. Let users set boundaries, review actions,  
or intervene. Giving people ways to steer makes the system feel safer, even if they  
rarely use them.

What we learned about trust in agentic AI
From our own projects we’ve seen what really shapes trust in agentic systems. These 
lessons didn’t come from theory, but from building and deploying agents in practice.
Honest answers build confidence
Trust starts with what the agent says. People value agents that don’t fall back on 
socially desirable answers but give honest, valid help they can use. Reliability beats 
politeness.
Agents can earn trust
We noticed that trust was there when agents behaved consistently and delivered 
useful results. It wasn’t automatic. Trust grew with every valid answer and every 
helpful action.
Humanity can trigger trust, but is hard to achieve
A human touch makes a big difference. Tone, empathy and natural interaction styles 
make systems approachable. But this is also where the most work is needed. Getting 
“human” right is harder than it looks.
The team behind the agent matters
Trust is not just in the interface. It comes from the people who design, train and host 
the system. Expertise and support structures give users confidence that the system 
won’t fail them.
Architecture matters. A fragile setup breaks trust quickly. But when systems are 
secure, monitored and well designed, users feel safe to rely on them.
Business context is key
Agents must understand the business they serve. When they fit into existing 
processes, use the right language and align with real goals, adoption is faster.  
Trust in agentic AI never comes from one factor alone. It’s the mix of valid answers, 
human-centred design, expert teams, solid architecture and business alignment that 
makes people confident enough to rely on agents every day.

Conclusion: trust enables autonomy
Agentic AI won’t win adoption because it’s smart. It will win because people feel safe 
using it. That means designing systems that communicate clearly, act predictably, 
and know when to pause, ask, or admit doubt. Trust isn’t a bonus feature. It’s the 
intersection between autonomy and usability.
